Anime · White Fox · Season 5

Re:ZERO goes round again

KADOKAWA announced on 14 November that a fifth season of Re:ZERO -Starting Life in Another World is in production, with an "ultra teaser" visual of Subaru and Rem and the tagline "For you, again and again." White Fox returns, with Masahiro Shinohara directing for the third season running, Masahiro Yokotani on series composition and Haruka Segawa on character designs.

The news comes right after season four, which aired in two parts this year: the Loss Arc from April and the Recapture Arc from August. Yusuke Kobayashi, Rie Takahashi and Inori Minase lead the cast again. Tappei Nagatsuki's light novels have reached 46 volumes, so there is plenty left to adapt, but there is no premiere date yet.

Via Anime Trending
Disney · Premiere

Hexed casts its first spell

Walt Disney Animation's original film premiered on 12 November at the El Capitan in Los Angeles, with mostly positive first reactions praising its art style and voice cast, led by Hailee Steinfeld, Rashida Jones and Jodie Foster. It opens in US cinemas on 25 November.

Via ComingSoon.net

The anime based on the classic RPG now streams with six episodes, then weekly from 17 November, with Spanish subtitles. Crunchyroll also licensed Medaka Kuroiwa season 2, which starts in Japan in January 2027.

On 14 November HBO surprise-released a scene from the start-of-term banquet, just after the Sorting. Janet McTeer's McGonagall tells John Lithgow's Dumbledore that Harry looks just like James; he answers that looks can deceive. Dominic McLaughlin, Alastair Stout and Arabella Stanton appear as the trio.

Season one adapts Philosopher's Stone, with Francesca Gardiner as showrunner and Mark Mylod directing several episodes. Season two, Chamber of Secrets, is already in production.

Film

US box office · 13–15 November

Ebenezer haunts the top spot

Ti West's take on A Christmas Carol opened at No. 1 with $19 million in North America and $42 million worldwide, Johnny Depp's first No. 1 opening in eight years. Audiences gave it an A- CinemaScore, while critics were mixed. Ian McKellen plays Marley, with Rupert Grint and Daisy Ridley in the cast.

The bigger picture

Paramount spent a reported $38 million on it. Amazon MGM's How to Rob a Bank, with Nicholas Hoult and Zoë Kravitz, flopped with $5.1 million on a $60 million budget. Next weekend belongs to The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping.

Via TheWrap · Seat42F

Music

Latin Grammys · Las Vegas

Rosalía's third crown

At the 27th Latin Grammy Awards on 12 November, Rosalía won five prizes, including Album of the Year for Lux, her third after El Mal Querer and Motomami. "La Perla" also took Song of the Year and Best Pop Song.

Silvana Estrada won Record of the Year with "Dime" and Aria Vega was Best New Artist. Spain also celebrated Alejandro Sanz's Best Contemporary Pop Album, while Jorge Drexler, Karol G and Milo J added more awards.

Via Deadline

Zelda · Nintendo Switch 2

Ocarina of Time breaks records

Nintendo says the remake sold 5.2 million copies, physical and digital, in its first four days (5–9 November), 810,000 of them in Japan. That makes it the fastest-selling game Nintendo has published on Switch 2.

In four days it already beat the lifetime sales of Echoes of Wisdom (4.09 million) and A Link Between Worlds (4.26 million). Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom remain far ahead.

Via VGC · Gematsu

Booker Prize · Serpent's Tail

M John Harrison wins the Booker

The veteran British writer won the £50,000 Booker Prize on 9 November for The End of Everything, a win The Bookseller called historic. Mary Beard, chair of the judges, described it as a "book that turns you upside down, raises a wry smile".

The prize had an instant effect: on 10 November publisher Profile Books ordered 50,000 more hardbacks, taking the print run to 148,000 including export paperbacks. The novel is also shortlisted for Blackwell's Book of the Year, a boost for a writer long admired in science fiction and fantasy circles.

Via The Bookseller
Barnes & Noble · Book of the Year

Whale Harbor wins the booksellers' vote

Mary Beth Keane's novel about 11 Irish brothers was chosen by B&N booksellers from 16 finalists. Craig Fehrman's history of Lewis and Clark won the nonfiction prize.

Via Parade
