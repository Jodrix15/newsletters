Anime · Wit Studio & CloverWorks · 2027

Spy x Family plans a double return

A fourth TV season and a second feature film were announced on 8 November at the Spy x Family Anime Extra Mission II event in Tokyo. Both are due in 2027, with no exact dates yet. Wit Studio and CloverWorks share production again, and Satoshi Kadowaki, a chief animation director on Attack on Titan, joins as character designer.

Takuya Eguchi, Atsumi Tanezaki and Saori Hayami return as Loid, Anya and Yor. Season three ended in December 2025, so the wait will be close to two years. The first film, Code: White (2024), took $95.7 million worldwide; next year it shares the calendar with Demon Slayer and Solo Leveling.

Via Anime Corner · Screen Rant
Box office · Warner Bros.

The Cat in the Hat lands softly

The long-delayed Dr. Seuss film, with Bill Hader voicing the Cat, opened with $20.5 million in North America and about $10 million abroad, second behind Godzilla. It cost a reported $86 million. Critics were cool, but audiences gave it an A CinemaScore.

Via Screen Rant · AP
Stop-motion · LAIKA

Wildwood keeps on walking

LAIKA's film dropped only 22% in its third weekend to $7 million, for $39.8 million in North America. With a budget of at least $100 million, it is still a long way from breaking even, but it keeps holding well.

Via AP · Fiction Horizon

Film

US box office · 6–8 November

Godzilla Minus Zero makes history

Takashi Yamazaki's sequel to the Oscar-winning Godzilla Minus One opened at No. 1 with an estimated $31.6 million, the best start ever for a foreign-language live-action film in North America. It almost tripled Minus One's $11.4 million debut, and Imax screens brought in $21.3 million worldwide.

The bigger picture

Made for under $20 million, it is GKids' biggest opening and already has about $63 million worldwide, with 90% on Rotten Tomatoes and an A CinemaScore. Overall ticket sales nearly doubled on the previous weekend. Hollywood's Godzilla x Kong: Supernova follows in March.

Via AP · Rotten Tomatoes
India · Worldwide release

Ramayana, India's costliest epic, opens

Nitesh Tiwari's first part, starring Ranbir Kapoor, launched on 5 November in five languages. It took $32 million in India over the weekend and $8.7 million in North America, the widest release ever for an Indian film. Part two will complete the story.

Via The Week · AP
Warner Bros. · 18 December

Dune: Part Three runs 151 minutes

Ireland's classification office lists Denis Villeneuve's adaptation of Dune Messiah at 2h31m, shorter than both earlier films (155 and 166 minutes). It opens the same weekend as Avengers: Doomsday.

Via /Film

Game music · Zelda

How Ocarina of Time got its orchestra

In Nintendo's Ask the Developer series, sound director Masato Ohashi confirmed that the Switch 2 remake's score is fully orchestrated, with newly composed cutscene music built on motifs such as the Kokiri Forest theme. Some tracks layer the original N64 recording under the orchestra.

The team studied the 1998 arrangements and found that moods shifted through pacing, not volume. Producer Hidemaro Fujibayashi said that, unlike Breath of the Wild's ambient score, Ocarina's music guides the player's emotions.

Via Nintendo Wire

Video games

Final Fantasy · Lucca 2026

Final Fantasy looks beyond remakes

Producer Yoshinori Kitase said at Lucca Comics & Games that once the Final Fantasy VII trilogy ends, Square Enix is "more likely to invest in new titles", though he did not rule out remakes or spin-off games and series.

Director Naoki Hamaguchi added that the first Remake, started by about 30 people, was the hardest of the three to make. In Revelation, due in April 2027, summon challenges move into the open world.

Via tbreak · GamingBolt

Sarah J. Maas · Bloomsbury

Maas breaks the UK fantasy record

A Court of Splintered Harmony, the sixth A Court of Thorns and Roses novel, sold about 189,000 copies in the UK between 27 and 31 October, according to NielsenIQ BookData. That makes it the fastest-selling adult fantasy novel there since records began in 1998, ahead of Rebecca Yarros's Onyx Storm.

Bloomsbury counts 1.7 million English-language copies sold across the US, Canada, the UK and Australia, and the book also tops Publishers Weekly's US fiction list. Older Maas titles are climbing too. The next ACOTAR book, A Court of Forgotten Melody, is due in January 2027.

Via Parade · The Bookseller
Prize · Baillie Gifford

Alexander the Great wins in London

Edmund Richardson's Alexander: God, King, Man took the £50,000 nonfiction prize, the first win for ancient history. He spent ten years on it, translating sources from 12 ancient languages.

Via AP
John Gwynne · Orbit

The Wolves of War begins a duology

Gwynne's new Wolfheart Chronicles draw on Slavic myth and the Rus Vikings. Its heart is Halia, a girl who runs with wolves, written as a tribute to his late daughter.

Via Winter is Coming
